## Composition of concrete 
- Limestone - sedimentary rock formed from marine organisms (coral molluscs), Chalk is a type of limestone. Formed from different crystal forms $CaCO_{3}$
- Lime - Calcium-containing inorganic material. 
- Clay - natural rock or soil with several clay minerals and other metal oxides in organic matter 
- Gypsum - used for wallboard, building plasters, soil additive. Added to cement to slow down reaction 
- Pozzolan - Naturally-occuring material containing silicone and aluminum oxides. When ground and mixed with water, will react with $Ca(OH)_{2}$ to form a type of cement. Abundant and commonly used in some countries. 
- Portland cement - most common type of cement. Named after Portland stone from a quarry in England. 
- Clinked - solid material produced in the manufacture of Portland cement as an intermediary product. Produced in a kiln from limestone, clay, and iron oxide. 

## What are Cement and Concrete?
- Concrete: A composite material consisting of aggregate particles bound together in a solid body by cement 
- Cement: A ceramic that by a chemical reaction binds particulate aggregates into a cohesive structure 
- Aggregate: Granular material which provide the majority of the volume of concrete. It is used as filler, providing dimensional stability and wear resistances. 
- Reinforced Concrete: A composite material consisting of aggregate particles bound together in a solid body by cement that includes a third reinforcing material. 

## Concrete Meso-Structure 
- Aggregates of varying shape and size, which constitutes 60% of total volume. 
- Cement: binding medium is a mass of hydrated cement paste (HCP).

## The Rotary Kiln heats limestone + other materials to produces clinker
- A kiln is a thermally insulated chamber to produce high temperatures for hardening, drying and chemical reactions 
- The raw materials are ground down, and enter the top of rotary kiln, which is slightly inclined. 
- Kiln can be 80-200m long, with travel times from 10min to 3 hours depending on the specific chemistry
- The directly flame and gases from combustion heats the raw meal to 1500 Celsius to complete the chemical reaction, producing clinker.

### Grind Clinker to make Cement Powder, and then hydrate and mix to produce Cement 
Once the clinker is formed in the kiln, it is ground down in the particle size to increase surface area for chemical reaction 

Gypsum is then added to create cement powder. The gypsum slows down the reaction to allow time for placing and working the cement. 

Hydrate with water mix: 
- Hydraulic cements harden by chemical reaction with water, and not by drying. 
- The reaction is exothermic, and heat is evolved 
- Two set reactions take place during hydration 
	- Setting (0-4 hours)
	- Hardening (4-8 hours)

## Concrete must be hydrated to cure and set over time 
- when concrete dries out, the suction forces developed when water evaporates from the pores in the concrete can actually crush weak cement paste 
- The concrete remains moist, the greater its strength and resistance to the suction forces. Means few cracks, less curling.

## Water content 
- Why is water needed? 
- Was is used to produce a fluid mixture which can be poured and is workable 
	- At a water/concrete ratio by mass below 0.4, concrete is unworkable 
	- Increase w/c ration increases workability, but too much may increase porosity 
	- Too much water may reduce final overall strength of material 
- Completes the required hydration reaction 
	- for complete hydration a w/c ration of 0.4 is required 
	- if w/c < 0.4 then unreacted cement powder is left.

## Plasticizers 
- Plasticizers are added to many materials to make the m more workable, flexible, and easier to handle. 
- IN cement they are also called water reducers. Can increase adsorption onto the surface of cement particles, less water needed to make workable 
- plasticizers can decrease the actual required water content, end result may have the same workability but with lower water content, therefore, higher strength.

## Fineness 
- Approx. 85-95% of cement particles less than 45 micron, with average around 15 micron. 
- Finness affects rate of heating and rate of hydration with smaller particle size increasing rate and accelerating strength dev. 

### Accelerators and Retarders. 
- Accelerators - increase hardening rate 
	- increase in early strength 
	- Some affect pH (bad for corrosion in reinforcing steel)
- Retarders - slow setting rate 
	- counteract accelerating effect of hot weather on concrete setting 
	- High temp cause increase rate of hardening which makes placing and finishing difficult. 

### Air Entrainment 
- Incorporating air bubbles helps concrete withstand freeze/thaw cycles 
	- Today air entrainment is specified for nearly all concrete exposed to sever freeze/thaw environments (pavement)
	- Water in the concrete mitigates to air bubbles and is frozen, stress associated with water expansion on freezing is minimized 
- Air entrainment agents 
	- introduce thousands of microscopic air bubbles into concrete 
	- Air entrainment decreases strength by ~20% at a given w/c ratio. Can decrease the w/c ratio to compensate.

### Supplementary Cementitious Materials
Waste products from other large-scale industrial processes can be incorporated into cement to generate different. 
1. Fumes of burning coal for electricity (fly ash).
2. By-product from the blast furnace during iron and steel-making (blast-furnace slag)
3. Byproduct during the manufacture of microchips and semiconductor devices (silica fumes)

Some of these products have similar effects to other materials (increase strength, improvements to chemical attack). Some of these act similarly to Portland Cement itself. 


### Mechanical Reinforcement 
- Reinforcing steel bar is commonly rebar 
- Rebar has a patterned surface to help create a good bond with concrete and transfer the load to the steel 
- Rebar is placed in regions of high tensile stress in the concrete, resulting in a strong composite structural material. 
- Failure typically occurs when concrete detaches from the rebar, such as due to corrosion of steel. 

### Steel Rebar works well chemically and thermally with concrete
- Corrosion prevention: alkaline (basic) conditions in standard concrete. 
	- Acts as a protective oxide layer on the steel to prevent corrosion 
	- Chemically which change the pH may result in corrosion 
- Thermal expansion coefficient: a between rebar and concrete are very close, which prevents cracking from occurring during large temperature changes.

### Pre-stressed concrete Done with Pre tensioning or Post tensioning 
- Pre-stressed concrete means putting concrete under compression before the structure supports any loads.
- This is done by inserting a high-tension wire (usually called tendon) in the concrete before it is used to support a load. 
- Two styles of pre-stressed concrete: 
	- Pre-tensioned - the wire is put under tension before the concrete poured and set. 
	- Post-tensioned - the wire is placed inside of a plastic duct or sleeve, and then concrete is poured around the duct. After the concrete is set, the wire is then put under tension. 
- In both cases, external anchors are used to keep the wire under tension. These create possible sites of corrosion. 
![[Pasted image 20251211000450.png]]
### Pre-tensioning 
- Wire is permanents bonded to the concrete throughout the beam 
- The anchors/abutments can be removed once the concrete is set. Sometimes the beam can deflect when the anchors are removed and the concrete is put. 
### Port-Tensioning 
- Wire is only bonded at the anchor points 
- Anchors are permanently fixed to the concrete.  The duct carrying the wire creates a channel where water can enter and cause corrosion. 
- The duct can be curved before concrete is set, to provide different force distributions.

## Alternative reinforcement - Fiber Reinforced Concrete 
- Principle role is to control cracking, and to modify behavior once the concrete matrix has cracked. 
- Effectively provides post-cracking ductility to the FRC, but does not add load-bearing capacity.
- Generally mixed and distributed throughout the material in low amounts (0.25-2%), and are short and closely spaced. 
- Can be steel fibers with hooked ends, or fibers made from glass and plastics selected to withstand the alkaline environment
