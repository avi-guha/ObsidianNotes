## Ferrous Alloys 
- Advantages: 
	- High tensile strength 
	- High temperature withstanding range 
	- Highly cost-effective 
	- Easily recyclable 
	- Can be alloyed with different elements 
- Limitations 
	- Relatively high density
	- Relatively low conductivity 
	- Poor corrosion resistance.

## Casting
- Liquid metal into mold 
- Casting 
	- Metal melted in furnace 
	- Most common and cheapest for making identical parts 
	- Gives good production of shapes
	- Overall weaker products with internal defects. 
	- Good option for brittle materials. 

## Casting - Sand Casting 
- Large parts. 
	- Cheap and easy to mold and withstands > 1600 Celsius 
	- Pack sand in desired shape 
	- After casting, machine can be completed for precision areas of part

## Investment Casting 
- Low volume, complex shapes
	- Pattern made from paraffin 
	- Mold by encasing in Plaster of Paris 
	- Melt wax and hollow mold left 
	- Pour in metal. 

## Die Casting 
- High production volume 
- Alloys with low temp

## Welding 
- Join two parts through metallurgical bond 
- Typically done with filler material (filler rod)
- Heat affected zone, region in which microstructure changed due to higher heat. 

## Machining 
- Process where desired shape or part is created using the controlled removal of material, most often metal, from a large piece of raw material by cutting 
- Machining is a form of subtractive manufacturing, which utilizes machine tools, in contrast to additive manufacturing, which uses controller addition of material. 

## Additive Manufacturing 
- Localized heating of metal using an electron beam. 
- Melted material is deposited in a known position creating custom shapes 
- Done in very high vacuum environments.
