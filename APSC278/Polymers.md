### Polymers 
- Long chain molecules 
- Collection of molecules in bulk form 

## Plastics 
- Synthetic or semi-synthetic polymers 
- Historically generated from oil or natural gas, but now also produced from wood fibers, corn, or organic materials. 

## Natural Polymers 
- Derived from existing biological processes 
	- Cellulose 
	- Sugars and starches 
	- DNA

## General Parameters for Bulk Polymers 
- There are dozens of common types of polymers with tens of thousands of variations and combinations all with different properties and compositions. 
- Polymers can be highly manufacturer-dependent, with proprietary different additives, making it difficult to copy products 
- Typically polymer synthesis info are trade secretes, and trademarks (name of products) become important to build public acceptance. 

### General Characteristics across all bulk polymers 
- Lightweight 
- Low elastic modulus 
- Low strength 
- Low fracture toughness 
- Good ductility, specific toughness 
- Highly formable 
This is for bulk material. In reality, many bulk polymers incorporate air to form useful foam materials. 

## Polymer - Structures and Definitions 
- Polymer - on chain molecules made up of a series of units called repeat units or mer. 
- Backbone of the polymer chain is normally a series of atoms joined by strong covalent bonds (covalent bonds are directional)
- The type of backbone and the addition of various side group distinguish the different types of polymers 
- Random rotation of covalent bonds in the backbone can lead to polymer chains with many bends, kinks and coils. 

## Mer units in Polymer Molecules 
- Idea monomer has the double bond. Polymer breaks this and we start to chain. 

## Polymer structural characteristics which influence mechanical properties

### A Molecular weight and degree of polymerization 
- During polymerization, polymer chains in a single batch will grow to different lengths - there will always be a distribution of chain lengths. There are two ways to characterize molecular weight 
	- Number-average molecular weight: determiend from  histogram of the number of chains within a given size interval. 
		- $M_{n} = \sum x_{i}M_{i}$
	- Weight average molecular weight: determined from a histogram of the total mass of chains within the given size interval
		- $M_{w} = \sum w_{i} M_{i}$
	- Note that $M_{w} > M_{n}$
- In all cases, weight average molecular weight is higher than number average 
- histogram for weight average is weighted heavier for longer, heavier chains. 
- Degree of Polymerization, average number of mer units in chain 
	- $DP=n_{n}=\frac{M_{n}}{m}$
	- m is the molecular weight of one mer unit 
- Many properties of polymers are linked to polymer chain length and molecular weight 
- Higher DP means 
	- Higher stiffness 
	- Higher melting point
	- Higher strength 
- Longer polymer chains mean greater VDW interaction 
- Greater thermal energy 
- High stiffness, and melting point.

### B: Linear vs. Branched chains 
- Linear polymers 
	- Mer units joined end to end in single chains 
- Branched polymer 
	- Side-branched chains connected to main ones 
	- Results from side reactions that occur during synthesis 
	- Chain packing efficiency is reduced, lowers polymer density 
	- Polymers that form linear structures may be branched. 
- Polymer branching affects the density and resulting properties of the bulk polymer material.

### C: Crosslinked and Network Polymers 
Crosslinked polymers 
- Adjacent linear chains strongly joined to one another 
- Achieved during synthesis or by a non-reversible chemical reaction 
- Often achieved by additive atoms or molecules that are covalently bonded to chains 
Network Polymers 
- 3-dimensional networks, mer units have 3 active covalent bonds 
- A highly crosslinked polymer may also be considered a network polymer 
- Materials have distinctive mechanical and thermal properties.

## Bulk Polymer Crystallinity 

### Individual Long Chain molecules
- very large strains are required to straighten out individual chains. 

## Polymer Crystallinity 
- Metals are 100% crystalline in solid form, or 100% amorphous in liquid 
- Polymer molecules are more complex as they involve separate chains of molecules interacting 
- Bulk polymers are often only partially crystalline 
	- Can range from completely amorphous to almost completely crystalline 
- Polymers form crystalline structures when side chain groups form VDW bonds with other side chain groups. 
- The density of a crystalline polymer is slightly greater than an amorphous polymer. 

## Amorphous Bulk Polymers can also be referred to as a glass-like state 
- in ceramics same small molecule is the base molecule for an ordered crystal structure Quartz, or a disordered amorphous state in glass. 
- Glass materials have their molecules distributed with correct short-range bonds but no long-range order. 
![[Pasted image 20251211020225.png]]

### Degree of Crystallinity is determined by polymer properties and processing 
- Factors inhibiting crystal formation 
	- Branching 
	- Extensive cross-linking 
	- Randomness 
	- Bulky side groups 
	- Atacticity (random distribtuion of side chain)
	- Chemically complex mer structure 
	- Speed of formation 
- DOP does not have strong effect on degree of crystallinity. 

- Factors of increase % crystallinity on bulk material properties 
	- Higher degree of crystallinity: 
		- Strength 
		- Stiffness 
		- Density 
		- Resistance to softening 
		- Chemical resistance 
	- Generally higher melting points due to higher density of secondary bonds.

### Thermoplastics, Thermosets, Elastomers 
Can classify polymers at high temperatures 
- Burns/oxidizes - Thermoset 
- Becomes softer at high temp - Thermoplastic 
- Elastomers - withstand large deformations and elastically return to original forms (most elastomers are thermoset)

### Thermosets - decompose when heated 
- Fabrication: 
	- Low molecular weight linear polymer prepared 
	- Next we cure it in a mold of desired shape using chemical activation (heat pressure UV)
- During the cure, chemical and structural changes generate irreversible cross-linked polymers or network polymers with covalent bonds. 
- The cross-linking and networking prevents the polymer from melting at high T 
- Generally thermosets are stiffer and harder plastics, can be used at higher temps than thermoplastics and more chemically inert. 
- Cannot be recycled. 

## Thermoplastics - soften when heated 
- At a molecular level, polymer chains separate not interconnected 
- Secondary bonds between the chains break with increasing temp 
- Amorphous thermoplastics formed above glass transition temp and can melt above the melting temp. 
- Allow them to be used in manufacturing for 
	- injection molding, etc. 
- Can be recycles

### Elastomers 
- Elastomeric materials 
	- Rubber-like elasticity  
	- Meets this criteria: 
		- does not easily crystallize 
		- Chain bonding rotations are relatively free 
		- Onset of plastic deformation is delayed (limited cross linking)
		- Must be above its $T_{g}$ for rotation and motion 

### Elastomers 
- Modulus of elasticity is small and varies with strain 
- Unstressed state - amorphous and composed of molecular chains that are highly twisted, kinked and coiled 
- When a load is applied - partial uncoiling, untwisting and straightening of chains in direction of stress 
- Release of Stress - Entropy increase restores the chains of original conformations

### Driving Force for Elastic Deformation of Elastomers 
- Entropy 
	- Thermodynamic parameter 
	- Measure of disorder in system 
	- increases with increasing disorder 
- Stretching an elastomer 
	- Higher strain values, chains straighten and align 
	- Entropy in the system can only increase if chains return to original coiled up position 
	- Basically cross-linking makes it want to return to original shape

### Forming and Molding Polymers 

- Thermoplastic materials - fabricated at elevated temperatures often by application pressure to shape stock material 
	- Fibers and Extrusions - produce continuous cross section 
	- Forming and molding - produce individual pieces 
	- 3D printing - custom fabrication 
- Thermoset materials - start in bulk polymer in liquid form can be final part when material is hardened 

## Fibers Drawing Polymers to increase strength 
- Drawing polymers can align the polymer chains in one direction 

### Extrusion 
- Squeezing pressing or pushing material through die to form product with consistent cross section profile 

## Injection Molding 
- Steps: 
	- Polymer granules melted 
	- Compressed by ram or screw 
	- Injected into a cold split mold under pressure 
	- Molded polymer is cooled below Tg
	- Mold opens and product is ejected 
- Excess material often pumped into mold to compensate for contraction 
- Cycle time can be seconds or minutes 
- Molds are complex and expensive 


### Blow Molding 
- Efficient for hollow objects with thin walls 
- High speed-cost effective. 

## Compression molding 
- Both thermoplastics and thermosets can be formed with this method 
- Compression molding can be done with softened sheets or viscous plastic placed in mold cavity 
- Overall pressures lower for injection molding so capital cost is less

### Vacuum Forming/Thermoforming 
- Sheets of plastic heated to a forming temperature, then stretched onto a mold. 
- Limited, cannot form around holes or surfaces beyond 90 degrees. 

### Blown Film Extrusion 
- Using air pressure instead of a solid mold allows for precise control of very thin film production

### Roto Molding 
- Used to make very large polymer structures 
- Mold is rotated around 2 axes, making material disperse and stick to walls of mold.
